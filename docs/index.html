TigrinyaLens

Does AI translate all languages equally well? We compared English-to-Tigrinya and English-to-French translations generated by the same multilingual model, then examined automatic scores alongside a small native-speaker review.

The research question

Does one multilingual AI model provide comparable translation quality when translating the same English material into Tigrinya and French?

Machine translation can improve access to education and information, but a fluent-looking sentence may still omit or alter meaning. TigrinyaLens focuses on measuring that problem rather than assuming that language support automatically means high translation quality.

Key distinction: the numbers above are chrF similarity scores against reference translations. They are not percentages of correctly translated sentences.

We also used an English-copy negative control and inspected low-scoring translations. A low automatic score was treated as a reason to inspect a sentence, not as proof that the output was wrong.

Human review

Three native Tigrinya-speaking team members independently rated the same 10 unique Tigrinya translations. This produced 30 individual example ratings. The previously reported overall mean was 2.5 out of 5.

The review considered meaning preservation, fluency and grammar. It is a small exploratory sample, not a representative survey of all Tigrinya speakers or dialects.

What the results do not prove

  • We evaluated one pretrained model, not every translation system.
  • The benchmark used one dataset split and 100 aligned sentence IDs.
  • chrF and BLEU do not perfectly measure meaning, fluency or usefulness.
  • Only 10 Tigrinya outputs received human review.
  • New sentences entered in the live demo are not part of the benchmark and do not change its reported scores.

Next research steps include more examples, more independent speakers, more domains, additional models and deeper error analysis.
